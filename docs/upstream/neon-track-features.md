Each uses a 1.6%-lap smoothstep transition at both ends. The shared road frame tilts road, kerbs and barrier positions; the centreline is lifted about the lower barrier so the road remains above the flat city ground. Sidewalks transition from the raised outer wall to the existing building foundations. Street furniture is omitted from those sloping pavement sections. Cars use their existing four-wheel surface fit; handling and AI code are unchanged.

The Undercity tunnel runs from 30.5–39.3% of the lap, approximately 330 m. Its curved concrete shell uses Monaco's flat-soffit concept, with a 25.1 m clear span and 7.2 m roof clearance. Walls remain outside the existing 12 m collision boundaries. Thick portal frames, weathered concrete, ribs, paired ceiling lamps, amber wall markers and a supported entrance sign make the covered section readable. Instanced structure/fixtures and the existing two local lights keep the lighting budget bounded. Camera obstruction testing includes the shell. Engine/rear-car audio uses the existing tunnel mix; rain is masked beneath the roof in world space, including views through the entrance and exit.

The single planar wet-road reflection is retained on flat sections and smoothly suppressed on banked normals. Those sections retain their standard road material lighting, rather than showing physically incorrect flat-plane mirror images. No new reflection targets or render passes.

## Dry undercroft, building context and rain ambience
The covered road now uses a separate matte asphalt material (roughness 1, metalness 0, environment intensity 0). A per-vertex weather mask removes the planar mirror layer inside the tunnel and blends wetness outside its mouths. Ceiling fixtures are spaced about 20 m apart instead of 12 m; emission and the existing local light intensities are reduced. No reflection targets or extra dynamic lights are added.

A continuous eight-floor complex rests on the tunnel shell, with perimeter columns outside the race walls, window bands, scattered lit rooms, front windows, cornices, rooftop plant and a taller central block. The procedural city reserves its footprint to prevent unrelated buildings overlapping it. The road passes under this mass rather than through a freestanding tunnel cover.

`external_assets/rain_sound.mp3` is the owner's supplied ElevenLabs recording (2.25 seconds, stereo 48 kHz). `public/circuits/neon-rain.mp3` is an unchanged copy with provenance recorded in the public manifest/credits. The original remains ignored. After the existing user-gesture audio unlock, Neon alone creates one rain source on the game's master bus. Decode-time equal-power tail/head overlap (220 ms) creates a continuous loop. The outdoor gain is .26; exposure ramps down over the last 18 m before entry and reaches zero inside, recovering over 18 m after exit. Master mute/pause controls silence it; disposal aborts loading and releases the source. No ElevenLabs API call or new generated audio was needed.
