Published derivative `public/circuits/neon-geisha.mp4`: 688×464, 24 fps, 10.04 s, silent H.264, fast-start, about 1.2 MB. Poster: frame at 2 s. Asset manifest and public credits record the supplied provenance, without claiming independent rights verification.

The 27 m tall light projection floats 12 m above Neon District's opening boulevard at z=-355. Its portrait is centred over the road; the source lettering remains alongside it. Additive blending removes black, softly feathers file edges, preserves the source's animated interference, and leaves the city visible through the signal. A suspended cyan emitter ring, billboard mist scattering and the existing wet-road reflection integrate it into the scene. No new shadow-casting lights or full-screen passes.

The video is created only for Neon, never the circuit menu or other circuits. It loops muted, supports inline playback, falls back to the poster if playback is denied, retries on input, pauses outside 550 m or while the page is hidden, and releases its decoder/textures/listeners on environment disposal.

## Distributed projections and spatial soundtrack
The final arrangement alternates four projections: geisha on the opening boulevard and at 48% of the lap; koi at 27% and 75%. Each pair shares one video texture/material/geometry, for two decoders total. Each new plane faces the approaching tangent and retains 12 m clearance. Every projection contributes to the existing wet-road reflection and mist lighting. The owner-supplied `external_assets/koi.mp4` is optimized to a silent 688×464 H.264 loop (about 777 KB) with a 3-second poster frame; the original stays private. Koi do not emit the geisha soundtrack.

The original owner's Grok soundtrack is extracted without speech generation or replacement into `public/circuits/neon-geisha-voice.mp3` (mono, 44.1 kHz, 96 kbps, approximately 118 KB). The published video stays muted; the soundtrack plays through the existing game AudioContext/master mix. Nearest-speaker selection limits playback to one voice inside 170 m. Distance controls gain and low-pass filtering; camera orientation controls stereo pan. Listener velocity projected toward the source controls pitch (1 + closingSpeed / 343, bounded to 0.82–1.18). Voice starts at the current video phase on entry; Doppler subsequently changes its timing naturally, so this is not frame-locked lip sync.

The final voice gain is reduced by one third (multiplied by 2/3) at the input, preserving the dry/effect balance. Original runtime effects: filtered 290 ms echo with 0.24 feedback and a seeded stereo 2.2 s decaying convolution impulse. Effects are routed through the existing master compressor/mute/pause path. Pausing stops the voice source; resuming starts a single source at the current video phase. Visibility uses the game's existing pause handling. Disposal aborts loading, stops the source and disconnects all added nodes. Other circuits never instantiate this audio graph.

Verification: TypeScript and production build passed. `scripts/verify-neon-hologram-audio.mjs` checked exactly two geishas and two koi, shared decoding within each pair, distinct video sources, 12 m clearance and exactly two voice emitters. Inspected both koi approaches in real Chrome. At 60 m/s test passes, approach ratio 1.167 and departure 0.833; mute/pause output RMS 0; isolated voice RMS .0362 and measurable post-stop effect tail RMS .00164. Reduced-gain assertion, far-range stop, repeated pause/resume and disposal passed with no page/console errors. Evidence in `artifacts/neon/hologram-audio/`. The preceding mobile production smoke test also verified tap-to-start audio unlock and no hologram-media fetches on the circuit menu. These measurements establish signal/routing correctness; final subjective mix preference is for the user's listening test.

## Visibility and departure mix adjustment
Koi now use a 20 m projection at 15.5 m above the road, 10 m lower than before, with 5.5 m bottom clearance; their emitter rings and mist lights follow the new elevation. Geisha placement is unchanged. Inspected both approaches in Chrome at 1440×900: the fish now sit within the forward view instead of against the upper screen edge.

A separate gain after the dry, echo and reverb branches fades the entire geisha signal behind the listener using exp(-(metresBehind/32)^2), smoothed over 80 ms. This retains the softer base volume and pitch shift on passing, while suppressing the lingering low-pitched voice and its tail. Travel heading is retained below 1.5 m/s so stopping after passing does not restore the advert's volume. Approach remains unchanged. Browser checks measured departure multiplier 0.0025 at 80 m behind versus 1 on approach; stopping behind retained the fade. Existing mute, pause, decoder sharing, effect-tail and disposal checks passed without console errors. TypeScript and production build passed.
